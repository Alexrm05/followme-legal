FollowMe Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 26, 2026

FollowMe is an app for driving in a convoy: the car in front (the leader) shares the route it takes, and the other cars get directions to follow it. This policy explains what data the app uses and why.

Controller

Alex Rodríguez Muñoz · followmeconvoy@gmail.com

We do not ask for your email, phone number or a password. We do not use your data for advertising or to track you across apps or websites.

Who it is shared with

  • The members of your convoy. They see your name, your position and, if you added them, your photo and vehicle icon. Only people who have the convoy code can join.
  • Drivers of other convoys nearby, only if you turn on "See and be seen by other convoys nearby" in the settings (it is off by default).
    • People who also have it on and are within the range they choose (2 to 20 km) see your colour, vehicle icon and position on their map.
    • They don't see your name, your photo, your convoy or where you are going.
    • This data is stored by areas of about 10 km in Firebase, where any FollowMe app can read it.
  • Google Firebase, which stores convoy data on servers in the European Union (Belgium, europe-west1), manages the anonymous identifier and receives crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics).
  • Other drivers using FollowMe, only if you send a road report (police check, accident, hazard or traffic jam): they see its type and place for 1–2 hours, never who sent it. Reports are deleted when they expire.
  • The DGT (Spain's traffic authority), only in Spain: every 5 minutes the app downloads its public list of incidents (traffic jams, accidents, hazards). The request carries neither your position nor who you are; the app then picks the ones near you.
  • OpenFreeMap, which serves the map images. It receives the area of the map you are looking at, not your exact position or who you are.
  • openrouteservice (HeiGIT, Germany), only when you ask for a direct route, to be taken back to the leader's route or to the destination: it receives your position and the points you are heading to, to calculate the route.
  • The place search, only when you search for a destination: Apple's on iPhone; on Android, Photon (komoot, Germany), a free OpenStreetMap search. They receive what you type and your approximate position, to show nearby results first.
  • Google, only if you paste a short Google Maps link (maps.app.goo.gl): the app opens it to read the location inside.
  • Your phone's map service (Apple or Google), to know which country you are in (speed camera warnings are only given where they are legal) and to name a point you pick on the map.

How long it is kept

  • Convoy data (positions, the leader's route, names):
    • Deleted as soon as the last member leaves.
    • If a convoy is abandoned without being closed, it expires 24 hours after it was created and is deleted automatically the next time any user opens the app.
  • Road reports: deleted when they expire (1–2 hours after being sent).
  • Points for confirmed reports: each point records which report it was and the anonymous identifier of the driver who confirmed it, never their name or position. Only you see them, and they last one year.
  • Profile photo and vehicle icon:
    • In the convoy, deleted with it or as soon as you remove them in the settings.
    • On your phone, kept until you remove them or delete the app.
  • Nearby convoys: your entry is updated every 15–60 seconds.
    • Deleted when you turn it off or leave the convoy.
    • If the app closes unexpectedly, it stops being shown after 2 minutes. It is deleted the next time you open the app or, after 10 minutes, when another FollowMe app looks at that area.
  • Anonymous identifier: deleted automatically after 30 days without use.
  • Crash reports: kept by Firebase Crashlytics for up to 90 days.
  • Usage totals: only daily sums with no one's data, kept for as long as the app exists.

Speed camera locations come from OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL) and are stored in the app itself: no data is sent to look them up.

Your rights

You can leave a convoy at any time, and your position stops being shared immediately. In the settings you can remove your photo or turn off nearby convoys whenever you like. To exercise your rights of access, rectification, erasure, objection, restriction or portability, write to followmeconvoy@gmail.com. You can also lodge a complaint with the data protection authority in your country (in Spain, the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos, aepd.es).

Children

FollowMe is not intended for children under 14.

Changes

If this policy changes, we will update the date above and let you know in the app.
