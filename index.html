FollowMe Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 19, 2026

FollowMe is an app for driving in a convoy: the car in front (the leader) shares the route it takes, and the other cars get directions to follow it. This policy explains what data the app uses and why.

Controller

Alex Rodríguez Muñoz · followmeconvoy@gmail.com

What data is processed

DataPurposeWhen
Precise location (position, heading, speed)Show your car to the members of your convoy, record the leader's route and calculate your directionsOnly while you are in a convoy, including with the screen off (iOS shows a blue indicator and Android a notification)
The name you chooseLet the other members know who each car isWhile you are in the convoy
Anonymous identifierKnow which car you are within the convoy without asking you for an accountCreated the first time you use the app
Road reports you send (type, place, time)Warn other drivers about police checks, accidents or hazards aheadOnly when you send one; they expire after 1–2 hours
Crash reports (what the app was doing when it closed unexpectedly, device model and system version)Find and fix bugsOnly when the app crashes; not linked to your name

We do not ask for your email, phone number or a password. We do not use your data for advertising or to track you across apps or websites.

Who it is shared with

  • The members of your convoy. They see your name and position. Only people who have the convoy code can join.
  • Google Firebase, which stores convoy data on servers in the European Union (Belgium, europe-west1), manages the anonymous identifier and receives crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics).
  • Other drivers using FollowMe, only if you send a road report (police check, accident or hazard): they see its type and place for 1–2 hours, never who sent it. Reports are deleted when they expire.
  • OpenFreeMap, which serves the map images. It receives the area of the map you are looking at, not your exact position or who you are.
  • openrouteservice (HeiGIT, Germany), only when you ask for a direct route or to be taken back to the leader's route: it receives your position and the destination to calculate the route.
  • Mapbox, only when you use FollowMe on CarPlay or Android Auto: it receives your position and the leader's to show the map and calculate directions on the car screen.
  • Your phone's map service (Apple or Google), only to know which country you are in, so speed camera warnings are given only where they are legal.

How long it is kept

  • Convoy data (positions, the leader's route, names):
    • Deleted as soon as the last member leaves.
    • If a convoy is abandoned without being closed, it expires 24 hours after it was created and is deleted automatically the next time any user opens the app.
  • Road reports: deleted when they expire (1–2 hours after being sent).
  • Anonymous identifier: deleted automatically after 30 days without use.
  • Crash reports: kept by Firebase Crashlytics for up to 90 days.

Speed camera locations come from OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL) and are stored in the app itself: no data is sent to look them up.

Your rights

You can leave a convoy at any time, and your position stops being shared immediately. To exercise your rights of access, rectification, erasure, objection, restriction or portability, write to followmeconvoy@gmail.com. You can also lodge a complaint with the data protection authority in your country (in Spain, the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos, aepd.es).

Children

FollowMe is not intended for children under 14.

Changes

If this policy changes, we will update the date above and let you know in the app.
